Le langage visuel de Legible

Design language proposal · built on IBM Carbon (Apache 2.0) · v0.1, 4 October 2026

Legi is the law, legible is readable. The product makes a decision readable by showing who is speaking in every paragraph and what the Court did with it. The design does the same: colour says who is speaking, a word and an icon say what the Court did, and the Court's own voice comes first.

1 · Logo

A four-line document. Each line has a margin tick in a different voice: the Court, the court of appeal, a party, the Court again. It is the product's core interaction in miniature, and it survives at 16 px. The wordmark sets legi in semibold to carry the name's meaning.

4 · The main screen

Two panes. Left, the decision, with a margin bar per voice and the Court's voice shown first (Cour seule dims everything else). Right, three tabs: what the Court held (its own sentences, quoted, no paraphrase), questions with paragraph citations, and the draft checker. 5 · Rules for the product

  • Colour = who is speaking. Icon + word = what the Court did. Never colour alone (≈ 8 % of men are colour-blind; decisions get printed in greyscale).
  • Margin bar, not highlighter. A 4 px bar per paragraph and a faint tint for non-Court voices. The Court's own text stays on white: it is what the lawyer reads most.
  • Court first. Default view dims everything that is not the Court. One click shows all voices.
  • Quote, don't paraphrase. "Ce que la Cour a jugé" is the Court's own sentences, verbatim, per ground. Interpretation stays with the lawyer.
  • Every claim has a § link. Click jumps to the paragraph and outlines it.
  • Confidence only when it matters. No percentages by default; an amber "à vérifier" tag only when a label is uncertain.
  • Review, not send. The checker proposes rewrites and "Envoyer pour relecture"; nothing goes to a client in one click.
  • French UI and French citation format (Cass. soc., 11 sept. 2026, n° 24-21.242, § 10). Confidentiality line on upload: zero data retention, no training.
  • Not DSFR. The French state design system (Marianne, République Française identity) is reserved for public services; a private tool using it would look official. Carbon gives the same sober, accessible, data-dense feel without that confusion.
